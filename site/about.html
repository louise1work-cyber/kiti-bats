Why it exists

It started with a simple problem.

Young cricketers do not get enough repetitions. A net session gives a player a handful of balls; a bowler gets tired; a coach can only watch one player at a time.

The KiTi-Bat was made to solve that — a rubber ball on an elastic string and a narrow bat, so a player gets hundreds of contacts on their own, in a driveway, without anybody needing to bowl at them.

The rest followed from the same idea. The KiTi Junior, because a five-year-old needs a shorter, wider face before they can handle a narrow one. The Catch-It Trainer, because fielding needs the same volume of repetition that batting does.

How we work
